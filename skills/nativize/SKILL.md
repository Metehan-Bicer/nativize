# nativize

A screen generated by a model usually looks like a web landing page squeezed into a phone. Every block is a rounded card with a shadow, the header is a gradient, labels carry emoji, text sizes are typed in by hand, and nothing behaves the way the platform does. People notice in a second, and so does App Review.

nativize fixes the craft and keeps the product. Features, content and flows stay. What changes is that the screen starts using the platform: its type ramp, its colors, its navigation, its lists, its gestures and its feedback. The goal is a screen that feels like it shipped from a small, careful team, and that only this product could have.

The rule behind every pass: **the platform is the design system.** Spend custom design on one or two moments of content people will remember (a chart, a verse, a photo, a timer). Keep the chrome quiet and native.

## Run it

1. **Find the stack.** `package.json` with `react-native` or `expo` means React Native. `.swift` files that `import SwiftUI` mean SwiftUI. Read the screen files the user named; if none, start with the first screen a new user sees (onboarding or the first tab), then the paywall if there is one.
2. **Scan.** Run `node <skill>/scripts/scan.mjs <project-root>` for a quick list of code-level tells with file and line. It is a starting point, not the verdict.
3. **See it.** If a simulator or emulator is available, capture before shots with `<skill>/scripts/capture.sh before` (default text size, the largest accessibility size, and dark mode). If the app cannot be run, work from code and say so.
4. **Name the tells.** Read [references/tells.md](references/tells.md) and list what this screen actually shows, worst first. Show the user that list before editing.
5. **Set a direction** (below), then fix in the pass order. Finish each pass across the whole screen before the next.
6. **Prove it.** Capture after shots with `capture.sh after`, then `python3 <skill>/scripts/compare.py before after` builds a side by side image. Check it at the largest text size and in dark mode before calling it done.

Stack specific fixes live in [references/react-native.md](references/react-native.md) and [references/swiftui.md](references/swiftui.md). Platform numbers (tap targets, spacing, type ramps) live in [references/platform.md](references/platform.md). Onboarding and paywalls have their own rules because App Review checks them: [references/onboarding-paywall.md](references/onboarding-paywall.md).

## Direction

Before touching code, write three short lines and show them to the user:

- **Category and feel.** What do the best apps in this category on the App Store or Play Store look like inside, not on their landing page? A budget app is calm and dense, a prayer app is warm and quiet, a workout app is bold and high contrast. If you can browse, look at real in-app screenshots of two or three leaders.
- **One accent.** Pick a single brand color from the product's world (its icon, its subject). Everything else is the platform's own label, background and separator colors.
- **The memorable moment.** Name the one piece of content that deserves custom craft on this screen. Everything else gets out of its way.

If the user already has a design system or brand, use it and skip what it answers.

## Passes, in order

1. **Platform fit.** Use the native navigation stack, the native tab bar and native sheets. Back must be the system back with its swipe gesture. Respect safe areas. Large titles on iOS root screens. Remove anything that imitates a system control badly (custom tab bars, custom back arrows, fake status bars, home made switches).
2. **Layout and reach.** Turn stacks of cards into grouped lists where the content is a list. One level of containers, never a card inside a card. Primary actions in the bottom third where the thumb is. Content scrolls; a primary button never gets pushed off screen. Test the smallest supported phone.
3. **Type.** Use the platform type ramp (iOS text styles, Material type scale) instead of typed in sizes, so Dynamic Type and font scale work. Cap scaling only where layout would break, never turn it off. One or two weights per screen. Sentence case. No all caps tracked labels unless the platform uses them there.
4. **Color.** Platform semantic colors for text, backgrounds, fills and separators, one accent for interactive things and selection. No gradients on chrome, no glows, no colored shadows. Dark mode must work and must not be an inverted afterthought.
5. **Icons.** One family: SF Symbols on iOS, Material Symbols on Android, or one consistent set across both. No emoji as icons or bullets. Icons sit at the text weight and size next to them.
6. **Components.** Native switches, pickers, segmented controls, menus, date pickers, search fields and swipe actions. Lists for lists, forms for settings. Toasts only for things that need no action; errors appear where they happen.
7. **Touch and feedback.** Tap targets at least 44 by 44 points on iOS and 48 by 48 dp on Android. Every tappable thing has a pressed state. Haptics only for confirmations and selection changes, not on every tap. Destructive actions ask once, in the platform's own dialog or action sheet.
8. **Copy.** Short, plain, specific. Buttons say what happens ("Save habit", not "Let's go! 🚀"). No "Unlock your potential", no "Supercharge", no exclamation marks in chrome. Empty states say what to do next.
9. **Motion.** System transitions and springs. Nothing pulses or loops in the chrome. Respect Reduce Motion. One purposeful animation on the memorable moment is enough.
10. **Onboarding and paywall.** Follow [references/onboarding-paywall.md](references/onboarding-paywall.md): honest pricing, a visible close button, restore purchases, links to terms and privacy, no fake countdowns, permission requests only when the feature needs them.

## Do not

- Do not change features, data, flows or the order of things the user did not ask about.
- Do not add a design dependency (UI kit, icon pack, animation library) when the platform already has the piece.
- Do not hide a problem by turning off font scaling or locking dark mode to light.
- Do not invent brand assets. If a visual is needed and no image tool is available, leave a clearly marked placeholder and say so.
- Do not claim the result was checked on a device or simulator unless you captured it.

## Checklist before you finish

- Before and after captures exist, or you said why not.
- Largest accessibility text size: nothing clipped, primary action reachable.
- Dark mode: readable, separators visible, no pure white blocks.
- Smallest phone: nothing overlaps, nothing is pushed off screen.
- Tap targets meet the platform minimum.
- `scan.mjs` shows fewer findings than before, and every finding left is a deliberate choice you can explain.
- A short summary for the user: the tells you found, what changed per pass, and what you left alone on purpose.
