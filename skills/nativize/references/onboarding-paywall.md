# Onboarding and paywall

These two screens decide first impressions and are where App Review rejects most often. Fix the design and the rules together.

## Onboarding

- Get to the product fast. One to three screens, each with one idea, or a single screen that shows the real app with a short explanation.
- Show the product, not claims. A real screenshot or the real component (a widget, a chart, a verse) beats a stock illustration.
- Every page scrolls if its content can grow, and the primary button stays visible on the smallest phone at the largest text size.
- Ask for a permission only when the user reaches the feature that needs it, with a sentence that says why. Never notifications, location and tracking all on the first launch.
- Skip is always available unless a step is truly required.
- Sign in only if the app cannot work without an account. If it can, let people use it first.

## Paywall

App Review checks these (Guidelines 3.1.1, 3.1.2 and 5.1.1). A paywall that hides any of them is a rejection risk.

- The full billed price and period are the most visible price. A weekly or monthly breakdown may appear, smaller.
- Free trial terms are explicit: length, the price after it, and that it renews automatically.
- A close button that is visible immediately, at a normal size and contrast.
- Restore Purchases is on the paywall.
- Links to Terms of Use (EULA) and the Privacy Policy are on the paywall.
- No fake countdowns, no fake "only today" discounts, no crossed out prices that were never charged.
- Products must load on every device App Review uses, including iPad if the app runs there; show a clear retry state when they do not.

## Design

- One plan highlighted by selection, not by a gradient. Plans as a native looking list or segmented choice.
- Three to five benefits as short lines with matching icons, no emoji.
- The primary button names the action and the price ("Start 7 day free trial", "Subscribe for $29.99/year").
- Same type ramp and colors as the rest of the app; the paywall is part of the product, not an ad.
